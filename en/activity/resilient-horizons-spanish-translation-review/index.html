Contributing to Resilient Horizons: Spanish Translation Review

Resilient Horizons: The Future of Landscape Architecture · ArquitectonicaGEO

I had the opportunity to contribute to the editorial process of Resilient Horizons: The Future of Landscape Architecture, a monograph by ArquitectonicaGEO, featuring an introduction by Beth Dunlop.

The book explores the role of landscape architecture in creating sustainable, resilient, and human-centered environments. Through ten projects developed by the Miami-based firm, it examines how landscape design can address environmental challenges, promote biodiversity, and improve the relationship between people and their surroundings.

Among the featured projects are the Pérez Art Museum Miami (PAMM), the Port of Miami Tunnel, Lakeside Village at the University of Miami, and Brickell City Centre. Through photography, site plans, and detailed narratives, the publication illustrates how landscape architecture can contribute to more resilient urban environments.

My contribution to the book involved reviewing, correcting, and enhancing its Spanish translation. I focused on improving the clarity, consistency, and natural flow of the Spanish text, ensuring that the language was appropriate for a publication dealing with landscape architecture and design.

I particularly enjoyed this experience because it brought together different areas that attract me professionally: architecture, publishing, languages, and editorial work. Contributing to the Spanish edition was an opportunity to participate in the preparation of a publication that explores the relationship between landscape architecture, environmental sustainability, and the communities these spaces serve.
